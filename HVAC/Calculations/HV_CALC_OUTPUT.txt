====================================================================================================

  CLOSED MODE = all valves shut, e.g. immediately post-detonation.  S-06 is
  explicit that it is A BRIDGE, NOT A SURVIVAL MODE.

  Occupied volume, airlock shut  185.0 m3          [R] = S-06's 185.0
  CO2 production at rest         9 x 0.02 = 0.18 m3/h    [C] S-06
  Rise to the 1.0 % CO2 limit    0.0096 volume fraction   [C] implied by S-06
  TIME TO 1.0 % CO2  (0.0096 x 185.0) / 0.18 = 9.9 h   [R]
      S-06 states 9.9 h  ->  REPRODUCED  [R]

  SODA LIME                      20 kg/day  ->  40 kg for 48 h   [C] S-06
  OXYGEN                         4.5 m3/day, store 2 x 50 L AT 150 bar (15 m3)   [C] S-06
  O2 store endurance             15.0 / 4.5 = 3.33 days = 80 h   [R]

  *** FINDING - HV-F1.  WHAT LIMITS CLOSED MODE IS THE SODA LIME, NOT THE O2. ***
      unscrubbed, CO2 alone .................... 9.9 h
      with the 40 kg soda-lime store .......... 48 h    [C]
      with the 15 m3 oxygen store ............. 80 h    [R]
      The O2 store outlasts the scrubbant by about 5:3, and both outlast the
      unscrubbed 9.9 h by a wide margin.  SO THE CONSUMABLE TO COUNT AND

      and it is the number a shelter commander needs.

  Against the 96 h design endurance: closed mode covers 48 of the 96 h.
  The other 48 h REQUIRE the filter trains to be running.  Closed mode is
  not an alternative to filtration; it is the bridge to it.

H.6   ROOM-BY-ROOM AIRFLOW  -  developed by this package
====================================================================================================

  S-06 gives the TOTAL, 300 m3/h.  It does not distribute it.  The split
  below is an engineering selection by this package [A], made on function
  and occupancy, and it sums exactly to the confirmed total.

  Nothing is supplied directly to bay 6: the airlock is the EXHAUST path and
  receives the whole 300 m3/h as transfer air, which is what makes the
  confirmed pressure cascade work (H.3).

  The 9 occupants are NOT in two places at once: they work in the ops room
  and they sleep in the berthing space.  A single fixed split would starve
  whichever of the two is occupied.  The distribution is therefore
  BALANCED IN TWO MODES with volume control dampers on those two branches,
  and the duct is sized on the worse of the two at every segment.  [A]

  U-01   EMERGENCY STORES / ESC 1       46.40 |    30 0.65  0 |    30 0.65  0 |     0
  U-02   LAVATORY + MEDICAL             28.80 |    30 1.04  1 |    30 1.04  1 |    45
  U-03   OPS ROOM & HAZARD PLOTTING     56.00 |   135 2.41  9 |    45 0.80  0 |     0
  U-04   BERTHING - 9 BERTHS            28.80 |    45 1.56  0 |   135 4.69  9 |     0
  U-05   CBRN PLANT + SUMP              24.96 |    60 2.40  0 |    60 2.40  0 |     0

  CHECK  day 300 and night 300 m3/h against the confirmed design flow 300 m3/h
         -> BOTH EXACT

  BASIS OF THE SPLIT  [A]
    THE OCCUPIED ROOM GETS 135 m3/h IN EITHER MODE - 135 / 9 = 15.0 m3/h per
    person, exactly the working-shelter rate S-06 names as criterion 2.
    The unoccupied one of the pair drops to 45 m3/h, which still turns its
    air over about 1.6 times an hour.
    U-05 CBRN PLANT gets 60 m3/h in both modes - it holds the filter trains,
        the CO2/O2 plant and the dehumidifier, all of which reject heat.
    U-01 and U-02 get 30 m3/h each.  U-02 is EXTRACTED at 45 against a 30
        supply so it runs slightly negative to the rest of the clean zone -
        the lavatory must not be the source of the cascade.

  *** PER-PERSON RATES, STATED PLAINLY ***
      whole shelter        300 / 9   = 33.3 m3/h/person   2.2 x the working rate
      occupied room        135 / 9   = 15.0 m3/h/person   = the working rate exactly
      unoccupied of the pair 45 / 9    = 5.0 m3/h/person   = the 5 m3/h survival rate exactly

      A FIXED SPLIT CANNOT DO THIS.  Without the two dampers, holding 15
      m3/h/person in both rooms simultaneously would need 270 of the 300
      m3/h and would leave 30 m3/h for the stores, the lavatory AND the
      plant room.  The dampers are not a refinement; they are what makes
      the confirmed 300 m3/h sufficient.

      If a reviewer requires 15 m3/h/person in BOTH rooms at once, the total
      has to rise above 300 m3/h and that is a change to a confirmed design
      value on S-06.  RAISED, NOT TAKEN.

      They are what turns the vendor's dirty figure into a maintenance

  *** WHAT CAN BE SAID WITHOUT INVENTING ANYTHING ***
  A buried structure surrounded by 4.7 m of rock is a very large thermal
  flywheel: 22.0 x 6.2 external, 600 walls, 900 roof and 600 mat of M35.
  Its steady-state condition is governed by the ROCK TEMPERATURE, not by the
  outside air, and the rock temperature is the missing number.  Until it is
  measured, a cooling load calculated from air-side data alone would be
  wrong in both directions and by an unknown amount.

  clearly assumed, and useful only as a bound:
    air mass in the clean zone   185.0 x 1.204 = 222.7 kg   [R]

    at 60 W/person sensible [A]   540 W  ->  8.7 K/h  IF THE STRUCTURE ABSORBED NOTHING

    at 100 W/person sensible [A]   900 W  ->  14.5 K/h  IF THE STRUCTURE ABSORBED NOTHING

  THAT IS AN UPPER BOUND ONLY.  It ignores the concrete entirely, and the
  concrete is the dominant term - which is exactly why the real answer needs
  a transient model and a measured rock temperature.  DO NOT SIZE PLANT ON

  THE THREE THINGS A BLAST VALVE MUST DO  [C] S-06, reproduced because they
  are the specification, not commentary:
      1  CLOSE FAST ENOUGH.  At 50 psi the shock front travels ~600 m/s, so
         in 2 ms it moves 1.2 m.  Keep >= 2.0 m of duct between the valve
         and the plenum and the leak-through is trivial.
      2  STAY SHUT.  The nuclear positive phase is 0.13-1.33 s - a thousand
         times longer than a grenade.  SPECIFY THE HOLD TIME, not just the
         closing time.  A valve that reopens at 50 ms is useless here.
      3  SURVIVE THE SUCTION.  The negative phase pulls the disc the other
         way at roughly 0.25 p_so ~ 86 kPa.  It must be rated in reverse.

      bay 5 at X 11098-12548.  The raw-air duct is 11.2 m long, so the
      2.0 m rule is satisfied more than five times over.  Recorded because
      it is the reason the trains are NOT put next to the valves.

H.12   THE RAW-AIR DUCT  -  a protective element, not a duct
====================================================================================================

  *** FINDING - HV-F2 ***
      S-06 puts the fresh-air blast valves in the WEST WALL OF BAY 1 and the
      NBC filter trains in BAY 5.  Between them the air is UNFILTERED, and
      the duct carrying it runs 11.2 m THROUGH THE CLEAN ZONE - bays 1,
      2, 3 and 4, past the stores, the lavatory, the ops room and the berths.

      Over that length the duct wall is THE ONLY BARRIER between the clean
      zone and unfiltered outside air.  A pinhole in it in the design event
      admits agent directly into the occupied space, downstream of nothing.

      THIS PACKAGE DOES NOT MOVE THE TRAINS - their position is confirmed on
      an issued sheet.  It specifies the duct as a PROTECTIVE ELEMENT:
        - fully welded stainless or heavy-gauge galvanised, NO push-fit and
          NO slip joints anywhere inside the envelope;
        - pressure tested to the ENVELOPE standard (+300 Pa) and not to a
          ductwork standard, witnessed, before any ceiling is closed;
        - run at high level on visible supports, NOT buried, NOT boxed in,
          NOT inside a ceiling void - it must be inspectable along its whole
          length;
        - labelled RAW AIR - UNFILTERED at not more than 2 m centres;
        - and it must be re-tested after any work in bays 1 to 4.

      ALTERNATIVELY, and this is the question for the protective designer:
      SHOULD THE TRAINS MOVE TO BAY 1, next to the valves?  Bay 1 is 2900
      wide against bay 5's 1560, it already holds ESC 1 and the 1000 L tank,
      and moving them would remove the raw-air run from the clean zone
      entirely.  THAT IS A PROTECTIVE-DESIGN DECISION, NOT A VENTILATION
      ONE.  It is raised, not taken.
