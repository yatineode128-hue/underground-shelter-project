G.7   GROUNDWATER  -  what 'not encountered' does and does not establish
====================================================================================================

  Report para 13, in full, verbatim:
    "Water table was not encountered in any trial pit. However, water table may raise during/after rainy season."

  P1 deck slide 29, verbatim:
    "Water Table not encountered in any trial pit"
    "Might increase during monsoon"
    "Proposed structure safe for water table at depth of 2 m below GL"

  THIS IS THE FIRST WRITTEN PROVENANCE THE PROJECT HAS EVER HAD FOR THE
  DESIGN GROUNDWATER TABLE.  Master A.6 and K.2 A2 record (-)2.000 as
  [ASSUMED - the single most important number to confirm] and give no
  source.  The source is now known, and it is this: no water was found,
  so a depth of 2 m below GL was CHOSEN and the structure was designed
  to be safe for it.  That is an assumption adopted in the absence of
  data.  It is a defensible one.  It is not a measurement.

  THREE REASONS THE OBSERVATION DOES NOT REACH THE DESIGN QUESTION:

  (1) DEPTH.  The pits reached about 1.5 m (G.3).  The design GWT is at
      (-)2.000, already BELOW the deepest pit, and the founding
      horizon is at -6.800.  A pit that stops at 1.5 m
      cannot find a water table at 2 m, let alone characterise one at
      6.8 m.  'Not encountered' here means 'not reached'.

  (2) SEASON.  The report itself says the table 'may raise
      during/after rainy season'.  The request is dated 20 May 2015.
      If the field work followed promptly it was done in the last weeks
      before the monsoon - the annual minimum.  THE REPORT NOWHERE
      STATES THE DATE OF THE FIELD WORK, so this is inference, and it
      is recorded as an open item, not as a finding.               [U]

  (3) GROUND TYPE.  Deccan Trap groundwater is not a simple water
      table in a porous medium.  It sits in the vesicular and jointed
      zones AT THE FLOW CONTACTS - which is the same feature master A.6
      already names as 'the hazard is not the basalt, it is the flow
      contacts' and which the red-bole differential-support case in B.3
      is built around.  Such water is commonly perched and strongly
      seasonal.  An open trial pit in the dry season is close to the
      worst available instrument for finding it.

  >>> FINDING SG-F6.  K.2 A2 STAYS [ASSUMED] AND STAYS OPEN.  The new
      evidence supports keeping it open, not closing it.  Master B.3's
      own arithmetic shows why this matters more than any other number
      in the project:

        of the 15.41 kPa/m lateral gradient, 9.81 is water and 5.60 is soil
        hydrostatic uplift on the mat    46.11 kPa = 6289 kN over 136.4 m2
        flotation FoS, mat cast only     0.33   FAIL
        flotation FoS, box complete      1.22   MARGINAL
      Water is nearly two thirds of the lateral load and the whole of

  WHAT WOULD ACTUALLY CLOSE A2, and what the programme currently does:

                  GWT (-)2.000',  20 d,  12-11-26 to 04-12-26,  TF 0

G.8   THE SOIL PARAMETERS IN MASTER A.6, CHECKED AGAINST MEASURED DATA
====================================================================================================

  Appendix B gives compaction and shear results for the murrum, which
  is the material the engineered fill, the side backfill and the berm
  will actually be built from.  Master A.6 and A.7.3 have carried
  round-number values for them since Rev D with no measurement behind
  either.  They are now checkable.

  (a)  UNIT WEIGHT AT PLACEMENT.  The fill is specified at 95 % MDD
       (master A.7.3, WBS A7010).  gamma_bulk = 0.95 x MDD x (1 + OMC).

  sample   class    MDD   OMC  0.95 MDD   gamma_d  gamma_bulk
  ------------------------------------------------------------
  45/15    GM      1.93    9%    1.8335    17.99       19.61
  62/15    GM      1.90    8%    1.8050    17.71       19.12
  65/15    GP      1.91    8%    1.8145    17.80       19.22

  MEASURED PLACEMENT RANGE          19.12 to 19.61 kN/m3      [D]
  MASTER A.7.3 uses                 20.00 kN/m3 for the 750 fill   [A]
  MASTER A.6 uses                   20.00 kN/m3 bulk, 21.00 saturated  [A]

       The assumed 20 is 2 to 5 % ABOVE what 95 % of the measured MDD
       gives.  For LATERAL load that is conservative and welcome.  For
       the COVER it makes the cover slightly lighter than assumed:

         750 fill at 19.61 kN/m3  ->  14.70 kPa  (assumed 15.00, -0.30)
         750 fill at 19.12 kN/m3  ->  14.34 kPa  (assumed 15.00, -0.66)
         six-layer sum falls from 39.15 to 38.49 kPa
         the RC1 declared allowance grows from 1.50 to 2.16 kPa

       AND THE OTHER WAY, which matters more.  Saturate the three
       soil-like layers and the cover gets HEAVIER, not lighter:

         gamma_d at 95 % of MDD 1.93         17.99 kN/m3
         void ratio e = G_s.gamma_w/gamma_d - 1  = 0.4999   [A] G_s = 2.75
         gamma_sat = (G_s + e)/(1 + e).gamma_w  = 21.26 kN/m3
         vs master A.6 gamma_sat             21.00 kN/m3   -> agrees within 1.2%    [R]

         750 fill   20.00 -> 21.26   +0.94 kPa
         300 topsoil 18.00 -> 20.00        +0.60 kPa   [A]
         150 filter  19.00 -> 21.50        +0.38 kPa   [A]
         layer sum on full saturation      41.07 kPa
         against the held design value     40.65 kPa   (+0.42)
         as a fraction of COMB 103 = 448.15 kPa   +0.09%

       >>> FINDING SG-F8.  The held cover value of 40.65 kPa brackets
           BOTH the as-placed case (lighter) and the fully saturated
           case (heavier by about 1 %, which is 0.1 % of COMB 103).
           C17's declared allowance turns out to be doing real work.
           NOTHING IS CHANGED: 40.65 stands, COMB 103 stays 448.15,
           no .std file is touched.

  (b)  SHEAR STRENGTH AND K0.  Master A.6 takes K0 = 1 - sin(phi) with
       phi ~ 30 deg.  Appendix B measured phi on four samples:

  sample   class  c kg/cm2   phi  1 - sin phi   vs 0.50
  ------------------------------------------------------
  45/15    GM         0.00   33d       0.4554   -0.0446
  62/15    GM         0.00   34d       0.4408   -0.0592
  64/15    SC         0.01   27d       0.5460   +0.0460
  65/15    GP         0.00   35d       0.4264   -0.0736

       Three granular murrum samples (GM, GM, GP) give K0 0.426 to
       0.455 - the assumed 0.50 is CONSERVATIVE against all three.
       One clayey sand (SC, TP-9, a 0.2 m surface layer) gives 0.546.

       Worst case, carrying the SC value to full depth, which no
       reading of the profile supports:
         gradient  K0.gamma' + gamma_w = 0.5460 x 11.19 + 9.81 = 15.920 kPa/m
         wall pressure at the floor (-)6.100
             master       83.18 kPa   [C] A.7.2
             worst case   87.11 kPa   [D]   +4.73%
         with blast 383 kPa on the same wall face:
             master      466.18 kPa
             worst case  470.11 kPa   +0.84%

       >>> FINDING SG-F9.  The measured shear parameters move the wall
           design load by under 1 %.  The walls are blast-governed;
           the soil is 18 % of the load and the soil PARAMETERS are a
           fraction of that.  A.6's K0 = 0.50 is not changed, and the
           report gives no reason to change it.

  (c)  A LIMIT ON ALL OF (a) AND (b), STATED PLAINLY.  On the report's
       own rockhead band the box occupies (-)2.000 to (-)6.800 and is
       therefore ENTIRELY WITHIN BASALT.  What bears on the walls is
       rock and the 300 lean-concrete annulus, not soil.  The master's
       K0-soil-plus-hydrostatic model is a conservative idealisation of
       that, and correctly keeps the full hydrostatic term, because
       water pressure in a jointed rock mass is real and is two thirds
       of the load.  The measured soil parameters are the right check
       on the berm, the backfill and the cover.  They are NOT a
       description of what the buried walls retain.

G.12   THE BLACK COTTON SOIL  -  where it is harmless, and the two places it is not
====================================================================================================

  Measured properties, Appendix A and para 14(a):
    depth               0.18 to 1.0 m
    liquid limit        61 to 63 %
    plastic limit       29 to 30 %
    plasticity index    32 to 33 %   [R]
    free swell index    60 to 65 %
    classification      CH  (IS 1498)
    SBC                 0.25 to 0.27 kgf/cm2 = 24.5 to 26.5 kPa

    A free swell index above 50 % is the top band of IS 1498's own
    scale - VERY HIGH swelling potential.  The deck's slide 30 puts it
    in plain words: the soil 'contracts and produce cracks in dry
    season, 10 to 15 cm wide extending to maximum depth of 1 m' and is
    'Not suitable for foundation'.

  WHERE IT IS HARMLESS - and this is most of the project.
    Nothing structural founds in it.  The mat is at (-)6.700 and the
    sentry footing F1 at (-)2.000, both in basalt, both far below the
    0.18 to 1.0 m black cotton horizon.  Master B.8.7's insistence
    that F1 bears 'on IN-SITU ROCK, never on backfill' is now backed by
    a measurement.  NO ACTION.

  TWO PLACES IT IS NOT HARMLESS.  Both are raised, neither is changed.

    (1)  THE COVERED ENTRY STAIRWELL'S STEPPED RAFT.  Master A.4.7:
         'Stepped RC raft 300 thk ON COMPACTED FILL', stepping from the
         top landing at 0.000 down to the platform at (-)2.000.  With
         300 of raft the top of that raft founds at about (-)0.300 -
         INSIDE the black cotton horizon at two of the report's three
         locations.  'On compacted fill' implies the black cotton is
         stripped and replaced, but NO SPECIFICATION ANYWHERE SAYS SO,
         no strip-and-replace item exists in the BOQ, and a very-high-
         swelling CH clay under a stepped raft is a heave problem, not
         a bearing one.
         The stairwell is declared EXPENDABLE against blast (master
         B.6) - but heave is a service-life problem, not a blast one,
         and this stair is the ONLY primary access to the shelter.
         >>> SG-F13 / open item SG-V6.

    (2)  THE CONCEALMENT LAYER.  Master A.7.3 puts 300 of topsoil/turf
         at the top of the cover, and CAM2 makes it the concealment
         layer, 're-laid from the site's own stockpile'.  If the site's
         own topsoil is this CH clay at FSI 60 to 65 %, then the
         concealment layer is a very-high-swelling clay 300 mm thick:
           - it cracks in the dry season, and a cracked patchy turf is
             a CONCEALMENT DEFECT, which is CAM2's entire subject;
           - the cracks are a direct infiltration path into the 150
             granular filter immediately below it;
           - wetting and drying cycles pump clay fines INTO that
             filter, which is the one thing the filter exists to stop
             (A.7.3: 'Stops fines clogging').
         The LOAD is unaffected - 300 at 18 kN/m3 = 5.40 kPa is right
         for a black cotton soil - so no calculation moves.  What is
         missing is a SPECIFICATION: either qualify the stockpiled
         topsoil against a swell limit, or import a non-expansive
         topsoil.  Neither exists.
         >>> SG-F14 / open item SG-V7.  BS1's 1:50 crossfall on the
         burster slab still works and is unaffected; it is the filter
         above it that sees more water and more fines than assumed.

       conservative, and the design does not use it - master A.6 uses
       the IS 1904 presumptive 3240 kPa - so NO DESIGN VALUE IS
       AFFECTED.  The slide is wrong; the structure is not.   SG-V8

       The report's murrum SBCs are 2.07, 4.27, 4.66 and 5.18 kgf/cm2.
       25 to 30 kgf/cm2 is the SOUND BASALT unsoaked band (30 to 36).

       ROCK strength to SOIL.  Nothing in the design uses it.   SG-V8

  (3)  'Compressive strength ranging from 609 - 900 kg/cm2 in unsoaked

       Appendix C's unsoaked results are [752, 814, 900].
