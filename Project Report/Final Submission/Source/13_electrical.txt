#chapter ELECTRICAL SERVICES | (SOURCES, LOAD SCHEDULE, DISTRIBUTION AND ESSENTIAL SUPPLY)

#head Sources of Supply

P1 Sources | Power is supplied from three sources:-

P2 Mains | The campus electric supply, through a meter panel, in peacetime.

P2 Generator | A 15 kVA diesel generator GEN-1 in bay 8, outside the gas-tight envelope and within the EMP boundary of the box.

P2 Battery | A 48 V battery with inverter, which supplies the essential loads on failure of both the mains and the generator.

#head Distribution

P1 Boards | The distribution consists of three boards, shown in Fig 13.1:-

P2 DB-M, Main LV Board | In bay 8, outside the gas-tight envelope, with changeover between the mains and the generator.

P2 DB-E, Essential Board | In bay 5, within the envelope, fed from DB-M and from the battery inverter on failure of both sources.

P2 DB-Z2, Zone 2 Sub-Board | Inside the shielded enclosure in bay 3, fed from DB-E through the pulse current injection protection on the Zone 2 boundary.

P1 Service Entry | Every conductor crossing the protective envelope passes through the service entry plate, with PCI protection on every power conductor and optical fibre for all signal circuits. No other cable penetration is made.

T The battery is housed in a cabinet in bay 5. Since the generator can run during the closed mode (Chapter 10), the battery is required to carry the essential load only during changeover and short interruptions.

P1 Non-Electrical Back-up | The two filter fans are fitted with hand cranks and the clean sump with a hand pump, so that ventilation and dewatering continue even if all electrical sources fail.

T The tank is of welded steel with a bund, contents gauge and low-level alarm to the panel. Its filling and vent pipes (DN25, metallic, with a lockable filler cap) are run up the existing generator air shaft SH-2 and bonded to the shaft earth, so that no additional penetration of the protective boundary is made.
