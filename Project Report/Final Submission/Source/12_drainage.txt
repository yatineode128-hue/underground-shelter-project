#chapter WATER SUPPLY, DRAINAGE AND SEWAGE | (WATER BALANCE, INTERNAL DRAINAGE, SUMP, SEPTIC TANK AND OPERATING PROCEDURE)

#head Principles

P1 Tanked Structure | The underground structure is tanked, not drained. Four rules follow from this and govern the whole drainage design:-

P2 Nothing through the Roof | No rainwater outlet, vent or soil stack passes through the 2.0 m cover, which would breach both the radiation shield and the roof membrane. There is no downpipe on the buried roof.

P2 One Service Crossing | All pipes and cables cross the envelope through the service entry plate on the north wall (X 11398 - 12198). The rising main from the sump uses it, and no other drainage penetration is made.

P2 Three Separate Streams | Clean water (seepage and condensate) goes to the storm soakaway; peacetime foul water goes to the septic tank and soak pit; decontamination effluent goes to a holding tank and is removed by tanker. The streams are never combined.

P2 Safety | No failure of the drainage may block an escape route, breach the envelope or flood an entrance.

P1 Water Supply | Potable water for the 96-hour occupation is stored in a 1 000 litre tank in bay 1, giving 27.8 litres per person per day. In peacetime the installation is supplied from the campus water supply.

P1 Clean Sump SU-01 | The clean sump is 1 500 x 1 500 x 1 500 mm internally (3.375 m³), with its invert at (-)7.600, and is cast monolithic with the mat. It has two submersible pumps, PU-01 (duty) and PU-02 (standby), each of 1.5 l/s, alternating automatically, and a hand pump PU-03 that needs no power. The control levels above the invert are: stop +300, start +900 and high-level alarm +1 200 mm. The duty pump starts about once in 3.4 days and runs for about 15 minutes, and the standby pump and the hand pump are therefore tested monthly.

#head Sewage

P1 Septic Tank | In the protective modes sealed-cassette chemical toilets are used and nothing is discharged. The septic tank serves the installation in peacetime and is designed to IS 2470 (Part 1).

T The tank has two compartments with a baffle at two-thirds of the length, inlet and outlet tees and a 50 mm cowled vent carried at least 2 m above grade.

P1 Soak Pit | The effluent from the septic tank is dispersed through soak pit SK-01, 2.200 m in diameter with an effective depth of 3.500 m, giving a side area of 24.19 m² against 22.5 m² required at an absorption rate of 20 l/m²/day. The pit is filled with 40 - 80 mm brickbat or stone, capped with 300 mm of sand and covered with an RC slab. A percolation test to IS 2470 (Part 2) Cl. 4 is carried out at the location before construction. The storm soakaway SK-02 uses the same detail.

P1 Decontamination Effluent | Effluent from airlock stages 1 and 2 is collected in the 1 000 litre tank TK-01 and is removed by tanker. It is never discharged to the ground.

P1 Changeover on Warning | On receipt of warning the following actions are taken:-

P2 | The lavatory is changed over to sealed-cassette chemical toilets.

P2 | Every trap is checked to be primed.

P2 | The clean sump is pumped down to its stop level.

P2 | Pumps PU-01 and PU-02 are confirmed on the essential board and the changeover is tested.

P1 Closed Mode | Seepage and condensate continue to arrive at about 400 litres per day with all blast valves shut, and the clean sump pump remains on the essential supply at about 10 per cent duty. If the power fails the standby pump is started, and if both pumps are unserviceable the hand pump is used. No opening is made in the envelope to drain water.

P1 Recovery | After the all-clear, the foul drainage is restored only after the ground outside has been checked, TK-01 is emptied before decontamination is resumed, the trap seals are checked while the envelope is still pressurised, the standby pumps and hand pump are tested, and the volume pumped during the closed period is recorded as a measure of the actual seepage.
