#chapter CBRN PROTECTION AND VENTILATION | (COLLECTIVE PROTECTION, FILTRATION, OVERPRESSURE AND CLOSED-MODE ENDURANCE)

#head Design Basis

P1 Principle | The occupants are protected against chemical and biological agents and radioactive dust by collective protection. All air entering the gas-tight envelope is filtered, and the envelope is held above atmospheric pressure so that any leakage flows outwards. The design basis is given in Table 10.1.

#head Filtration

P1 Threats and Filter Stages | Particulate agents and gaseous agents are removed by different mechanisms, and the filter stages are therefore in series, as given in Table 10.2.

#table Threats and Means of Removal
#widths 30,20,50
Threat | Form | Removed by
Radioactive dust, fallout, biological agents | Aerosol, 0.1 to 10 μm | HEPA filter, EN 1822 class H14, 99.995 % at the most penetrating particle size
Chemical warfare agents | Vapour | Impregnated activated carbon (ASZM-TEDA)
Coarse dust; the blast pulse | - | Pre-filter; blast valve
#end

P1 Filter Train | Each filter train consists of seven stages in the order shown in Fig 10.1: weather louvre with sand and debris trap; blast valve (closes in less than 2 ms and holds for 1.3 s); pre-filter G4/F7; HEPA filter H14; activated carbon bed ASZM-TEDA (300 000 mg.min/m³); fan with electric drive and hand crank; and supply plenum at +50 to +100 Pa. A differential pressure gauge is fitted across every stage and a flow meter on each train, to indicate filter loading and to set the change-out of each stage. The fan is selected on the pressure drop of the filters in the dirty condition, and the hand crank is sized for the same duty.

P1 Carbon Beds | The activated carbon beds give out heat when they adsorb, and the filter bay is therefore included in the fire load of the shelter (Chapter 14). The plant room is separated from the airlock by the fire and gas-tight wall W5.

#head Air Distribution and Overpressure

P1 Air Path | Fresh air is drawn through the intake shaft SH-1 (600 x 600 mm, gooseneck head at +1.500, west of the box), enters through BV-1 and BV-2 in the west wall of bay 1, and is carried along the clean zone to the filter trains in bay 5 in a raw-air duct. The raw-air duct is fully welded with no push-fit or slip joints, is run exposed along its whole length and labelled, and is tested to the +300 Pa envelope standard. Filtered air is distributed to the rooms, flows through the three stages of the airlock and leaves through BV-3 into the stair shaft. The air path is shown in Fig 10.2.

P1 Room Distribution | The operations room and the berthing bay are not occupied at the same time, and two volume control dampers switch the flow between day and night settings, as given in Table 10.4. The occupied room of the pair receives 135 m³/h, i.e. 15 m³/h per person.

P1 Decontamination Airlock | The airlock has three stages, 2 000 x 2 000, 2 000 x 1 500 and 2 000 x 1 500 mm. Each entry requires five air changes of the airlock volume before the next person can pass.

#calc
Purge volume = 5 x (2.0 x 2.0 x 3.2) ;; 64 m³
Purge time at 300 m³/h ;; 12.8 min
Entry rate through the airlock ;; 4 to 5 persons per hour
#end

P1 Trap Seals | Every water trap within the envelope is a pressure boundary. Traps are provided with 75 mm deep seals, which hold 736 Pa, i.e. 7.4 times the operating overpressure and 2.5 times the test pressure, and are kept primed as a routine maintenance task.

#head Closed Mode

P1 Endurance | In the closed mode all five blast valves are shut and there is no fresh air. The endurance is set by the carbon dioxide produced and the oxygen consumed by the occupants.

#calc
Allowable CO<sub>2</sub> rise 0.04 % to 1.0 % = 0.0096 volume fraction; occupied volume 184.96 m³
CO<sub>2</sub> produced = 9 x 0.020 m³/h ;; 0.18 m³/h
Time to reach 1.0 % without scrubbing = 0.0096 x 184.96 / 0.18 ;; 9.9 h
CO<sub>2</sub> scrubber, soda lime store 40 kg at 20 kg/day ;; 48 h
Oxygen store, 2 x 50 litre cylinders at 150 bar = 15 m³ at 4.5 m³/day ;; 80 h
#end

T The CO<sub>2</sub> scrubber is therefore an essential item, and the closed mode can be maintained for 48 hours on the soda lime store. For the remainder of the 96 hours the shelter operates in the filtered mode.

P1 CO<sub>2</sub> Scrubber Loop | The recirculation flow through the scrubber, Q = CO<sub>2</sub> production / (efficiency x target concentration), is 72.0 m³/h at 50 per cent single-pass efficiency and a target of 0.5 per cent. A recirculation loop of **75 m³/h** has been adopted. Its fan draws about 11.6 W at 250 Pa, within the allowance of 0.10 kW on the essential board.

P1 Operating Modes | The operating modes are given in Table 10.6. The generator air path through BV-4 and BV-5 serves bay 8 only, which is outside the gas-tight envelope. All five blast valves close on the arrival of the shock, and BV-4 and BV-5 are then reopened for generator operation, so that the generator can run in the closed mode without affecting the clean zone.

#widths 26,74

#head Heat Balance

P1 Heat Gains | The sensible heat gain within the envelope in the closed mode is the electrical load dissipated inside it (5.733 kW) and the sensible heat of the occupants (9 x 70 W = 0.630 kW), a total of 6.363 kW, or 2.199 GJ over 96 hours.

P1 Heat Sink | Almost all of this heat is absorbed by the concrete of the envelope and the rock around it, since the air in the envelope has a heat capacity of only 262 kJ/K. The concrete that can respond within 96 hours (a thermal penetration depth of √(αt) = 0.436 m) has been taken as a 0.30 m layer over the 414.38 m² of internal surface, with a heat capacity of 273 493 kJ/K.

P1 Cooling Duty | To hold the internal air at 30 °C from a ground temperature of 26 °C, the structure absorbs 273 493 x 4 = 1.094 GJ and the remaining 1.105 GJ is to be removed, a mean sensible duty of 3.20 kW. With a latent load of 0.405 kW the mean duty is 3.60 kW (1.02 TR), and a closed-mode cooling duty of **4 kW (1.14 TR)** has been adopted.

#head Testing

P1 Envelope Leakage Test | On completion, the gas-tight envelope is tested at +300 Pa. The leakage must not exceed 0.15 volume changes per hour (32.5 m³/h). The test is repeated after any work that penetrates the envelope, and the raw-air duct is re-tested after any work in bays 1 to 4.
