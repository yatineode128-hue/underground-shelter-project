#head Blast Loading

P1 Blast Wave | A nuclear air burst releases its energy far faster than the surrounding air can move away, and the disturbance steepens into a shock front across which pressure, density and particle velocity rise almost instantaneously. Two pressures act behind the front:-

P2 Static Overpressure, p<sub>so</sub> | The rise above ambient pressure, acting equally in all directions. It loads the roof and walls of a buried structure.

P2 Dynamic Pressure, q | The kinetic pressure of the moving air, q = 0.5 ρ u², which acts only in the direction of flow and produces drag on an object standing in the flow.

P1 Design Blast Parameters | The design blast parameters are as under. The reflected pressure applies only to a free-standing face normal to the wave and is used in Chapter 9 for the sentry post.

#calc
Peak incident static overpressure, p<sub>so</sub> (50 psi) ;; 344.7 kPa
Positive phase duration, t<sub>d</sub> ;; 0.13 to 1.33 s
Peak reflected pressure, p<sub>r</sub> ;; 1 366 kPa
Peak dynamic pressure, q ;; 282 kPa
Reflection coefficient, p<sub>r</sub> / p<sub>so</sub> = 1366 / 344.7 ;; 3.96
#end

#fig fig_blast_wave | Design Blast Wave (Pressure-Time History)

P1 Load on a Buried Structure | Soil transmits the air-blast pressure to a buried structure as a stress wave. IS 4991 Cl. 7.2 and Table 3 apply a soil transmission factor K<sub>a</sub> to the free-field overpressure. The founding horizon is below the design water table, and for saturated soil K<sub>a</sub> = 1.0. The roof and walls of the box are therefore designed for the full overpressure. No reduction has been taken for arching of the soil over the roof, which is conservative.

P1 Dynamic Response | A blast-loaded slab responds essentially in its first mode, and is idealised as a single degree of freedom system. Its response depends on the ratio of the load duration to its natural period, t<sub>d</sub> / T: below about 0.1 the response is impulsive, between 0.1 and 10 it is dynamic, and above 10 it is quasi-static, i.e. the peak response approaches that under a suddenly applied constant load. The natural period of the governing element, the 900 mm pressure slab spanning 5.0 m, is worked out below.

#calc
Participating mass, m = 0.900 x 2500 + 0.25 x 2.0 x 2000 ;; 3 250 kg/m²
Cracked stiffness, 0.5 E I<sub>g</sub> = 0.5 x 2.958 x 10<sup>10</sup> x 0.900³ / 12 ;; 8.985 x 10<sup>8</sup> Nm²/m
f<sub>1</sub> = (22.373 / 2π) √(EI / m L<sub>n</sub><sup>4</sup>), fixed-fixed beam ;; 74.9 Hz
T = 1 / f<sub>1</sub> ;; 13.4 ms
!t<sub>d</sub> / T = 130 / 13.4 to 1330 / 13.4 ;; 10 to 100
#end

T The ratio t<sub>d</sub> / T lies between 10 and 100 and the response is **quasi-static** (Fig 5.2). An equivalent static load obtained with a dynamic load factor therefore gives the correct design demand.

#fig fig_regimes | Response Regime of the Structure

P1 Dynamic Load Factor | For an elasto-plastic system under a suddenly applied load, the dynamic load factor is related to the ductility ratio μ (maximum displacement / yield displacement) by IS 4991 Cl. 10.3.3.

#calc
DLF = μ / (μ - 0.5); check: μ = 1 gives DLF = 2.00, the elastic value
Adopted μ = 5 (moderate, repairable damage), DLF = 5 / 4.5 ;; 1.111
!Design blast pressure = 344.7 x 1.111 ;; 383 kPa
#end

T The design pressure of **383 kPa** is applied to the roof and to all external walls. The ductility on which it depends is ensured by the detailing: the neutral axis depth of the pressure slab is x<sub>u</sub>/d = 0.139 against the limit of 0.46 for Fe500, and all blast-governed elements carry designed shear links (Chapter 7).

#fig fig_dlf | Dynamic Load Factor against Ductility Ratio

P1 Plastic Mechanism Moment | The flexural demand on the blast-governed one-way elements is taken as the collapse mechanism moment of a fixed-fixed strip, M<sub>p</sub> = w L<sub>n</sub>² / 16, which is reached when hinges have formed at both supports and at mid-span. This is permissible because the sections are ductile enough to redistribute moment, which is the same condition on which μ = 5 depends.

P1 Burster Slab to Falls | The burster slab is laid to a 1 in 50 crossfall from the box centreline (Y = 3100) towards both edges, parallel to the finished grade. Water that passes the topsoil is intercepted by the granular filter and led to the berm toe without any pipe in the cover. The fall is taken up in the compacted fill, which is 750 mm at the crown and 688 mm at the edges, so the load does not increase anywhere.

P1 Headhouse Loads | The headhouse roof, which is flush with the berm crest and has no earth cover, carries 383 + 12.5 (self weight) + 1.0 (SIDL) = **396.5 kPa**. The headhouse walls stand behind about 2.5 m of berm; since earth transmits pressure and the transmission factor of a dry compacted berm cannot be relied upon, the walls are designed for the full **383 kPa** instead of the drag load of C<sub>d</sub> q = 0.4 x 282 = 113 kPa. The headhouse also fills with blast pressure through the entry, so the walls are designed for 383 kPa acting on either face.

#head Seismic and Wind Loads

P1 Underground Structure | The seismic load on the buried box has been worked out to IS 1893 (Part 1) : 2016.

#calc
Z = 0.16 (Zone III), I = 1.5, R = 4.0, S<sub>a</sub>/g = 2.5
A<sub>h</sub> = (Z/2)(I/R)(S<sub>a</sub>/g) = 0.08 x 0.375 x 2.5 ;; 0.075
Seismic weight W ;; 14 002 kN
V<sub>b</sub> = 0.075 x 14 002 ;; 1 050 kN
Shear per long wall = 525 kN; τ = 525 x 10³ / (600 x 0.8 x 22 000) ;; 0.05 N/mm²
#end

T The seismic shear stress in the walls is negligible, and the boundary element provisions of IS 13920 Cl. 10.4 are not attracted. A buried box moves with the surrounding ground and seismic loading does not govern any element of it.

P1 Sentry Post | The seismic load on the sentry post has been worked out to IS 1893 (Part 1) : 2016. The response reduction factor is taken as 3.0, since the masonry infill is built tight to the frame and is not separated from it.

#calc
Z = 0.16, I = 1.5, R = 3.0, S<sub>a</sub>/g = 2.5 (rock, T on the plateau)
A<sub>h</sub> = 0.08 x 0.50 x 2.5 ;; 0.100
T<sub>a</sub>, bare frame = 0.075 h<sup>0.75</sup> = 0.075 x 6.250<sup>0.75</sup> ;; 0.297 s
T<sub>a</sub>, with infill = 0.09 h / √d, X / Z direction ;; 0.281 / 0.252 s
Seismic weight from the STAAD model, roof 331.46 + floor 400.34 ;; 731.80 kN
!Design base shear V<sub>b</sub> = 0.100 x 731.80 ;; 73.18 kN
Check from the support reactions: 18.295 kN per column x 4 ;; 73.18 kN
#end

T All three period estimates lie on the plateau of the spectrum (0.10 to 0.40 s). A hand calculation of the seismic weight gives 592.7 kN; the model value of 731.80 kN is higher because it includes half the first-storey infill at the floor level and the full beam depth in the self weight, and the design uses the higher value.
