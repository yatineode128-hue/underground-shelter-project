#chapter PLANNING AND ARCHITECTURAL LAYOUT | (UNDERGROUND STRUCTURE, HEADHOUSE, ENTRY AND SENTRY POST)

#head Planning Concept

P1 Protective Boundary | The planning of the installation is governed by the protective boundary, which is the continuous surface that excludes blast, contaminated air and radiation. The boundary is formed by **Blast Doors 1 and 2 at level (-)6.100, walls W6 and W7 in which they are set, the perimeter walls, the mat foundation and the pressure (roof) slab**. The stair shaft, the headhouse and the covered entry stairwell lie outside the boundary. The stair shaft is open to the atmosphere through the stair void and fills with blast pressure; walls W6 and W7 and both blast doors are therefore designed for the full blast pressure.

P1 Gas-Tight Envelope | Within the protective boundary, bays 1 to 6 form the gas-tight envelope, which is held at a positive pressure by the filtration plant. Its floor area is 67.80 m² and its volume 216.96 m³. Bay 7 (stair shaft) and bay 8 (generator) are outside the gas-tight envelope; bay 8 is a grey zone, separately ventilated for the generator.

P1 Planning Principles | The following principles have been followed in the layout:-

P2 Width Fixed, Length Free | The roof spans one way across the 5.0 m internal width (Chapter 7), so its moment depends only on the width. Additional accommodation is therefore obtained by lengthening the box, never by widening it.

P2 Minimum Penetrations | Every opening in the boundary is a blast, gas and EMP discontinuity. All services enter through one service entry plate, the air enters and leaves through blast valves only, and nothing penetrates the buried roof.

P2 No Movement Joints | No movement joint is provided anywhere within the protective envelope. Construction joints are made with continuous reinforcement, two waterstops and a welded EMP bonding strap.

P2 Clean to Dirty | The bays are arranged so that air, people and water move from the clean end (west) towards the dirty end (east), through the decontamination airlock to the stair shaft.

#head Headhouse and Covered Entry Stairwell

P1 Entry Headhouse | The headhouse is a reinforced concrete room standing on the pressure slab over the stair shaft, as under:-

P2 Dimensions | External 4 800 x 5 800 mm (X 13600 - 18400, Y 200 - 6000); internal 4 000 x 5 000 mm; clear height 2 400 mm.

P2 Construction | Walls HW1 (south), HW2 (north), HW3 (west) and HW4 (east) are 400 mm thick; the roof is 500 mm thick with its soffit at +0.400 and top at +0.900, without earth cover. The floor is the top of the pressure slab at (-)2.000. The berm is graded against all four walls to +0.900 at 1.5 : 1.

P2 Opening | The inner security door, 900 x 2 100 mm, is in HW2 at X 14450 - 15350. It is a security door and is not blast rated.

P2 Support | HW1 and HW2 stand directly over the box perimeter walls and HW4 over wall W7. HW3 stands on the pressure slab over bay 6 and is designed as a line load on the slab (Chapter 8).

P1 Covered Entry Stairwell | The covered entry stairwell leads from ground level to the headhouse platform, as under:-

P2 Dimensions | External 6 800 x 2 000 mm (X 9250 - 16050, Y 5750 - 7750); internal 6 300 x 1 500 mm.

P2 Construction | 250 mm RC side walls, headwall and east wall; top landing at 0.000 (X 9500 - 11000); flight of 12 risers of 166.67 mm with 11 goings of 300 mm (X 11000 - 14300), waist 250 mm; platform 1 500 x 1 500 mm at (-)2.000 (X 14300 - 15800); raking roof 250 mm with soffit 2 200 mm above the flight, remaining 250 mm over the platform.

P2 Entry | The entry door, 1 000 x 2 100 mm, is at grade in the headwall and opens outward. A 300 mm channel with grating across the full 1 500 mm width at the threshold intercepts surface water.

P2 Foundation | The stairwell stands on a stepped RC raft 300 mm thick on compacted granular fill, with a movement joint where it meets the headhouse. A 1.0 m³ external sump at the platform drains to its own soakaway. The berm is graded against the walls at 1.5 : 1 to +0.900.

P1 Function | The covered stairwell and the headhouse provide a weather-proof and secure approach to the stair shaft. Both are outside the protective boundary. The headhouse roof and walls are designed for the full blast pressure; the covered stairwell is designed for static loads only and is not blast rated.

#head Radiation Shielding

P1 Engineered Cover | The roof of the box is covered by 2.0 m of engineered cover in six layers (Chapter 5). Since a buried roof receives the full blast overpressure (K<sub>a</sub> = 1.0), the cover is not provided for blast; it is provided for protection against nuclear radiation, as under:-

P2 Fallout | The first metre of cover gives a protection factor of about 2 200 against fallout, against a requirement of the order of 1 000.

P2 Initial Radiation | The second metre provides the mass needed to attenuate the initial neutron and gamma radiation. Attenuation is exponential in the areal density of the shield, and the moisture in the fill and the bound water in the concrete are effective in slowing neutrons.

P2 Burster Slab | A 200 mm M30 reinforced concrete burster slab (T12 at 150 mm both ways) within the cover breaks up a penetrating item so that its energy is dispersed in the 500 mm layer of crushed basalt rubble below it instead of being delivered to the roof as a concentrated load.
