#chapter STRUCTURAL ANALYSIS | (ANALYSIS MODELS AND METHODS)

#head Approach

P1 Methods Used | The structural analysis has been carried out as under:-

P2 Underground Structure | The box has been modelled in STAAD.Pro as a plate model on an elastic foundation to represent the load distribution and the support conditions. The elements have been designed by hand calculation, using the plastic mechanism moment under the equivalent static blast load for the governing combination 103 and the elastic coefficients of IS 456 for the other cases.

P2 Stairs, Headhouse and Stairwell | Designed as one-way slabs and walls by hand calculation, with the headhouse roof checked by three methods (Chapter 8). The covered entry stairwell has also been modelled in STAAD.Pro for the static load cases.

P2 Sentry Post | Analysed as a three-dimensional space frame in STAAD.Pro for the gravity and seismic load cases. The member forces used in design have been obtained from the model and from the portal method, the larger value being adopted.

P1 Flotation | The springs of an elastic foundation model act in tension as well as compression, so the model cannot show whether the box floats. Flotation has therefore been checked by hand on the actual weights and the actual plan area (Chapter 7).

#head Plastic Mechanism Method

P1 Principle | For a one-way strip fixed at both ends, the elastic moments are w L²/12 at the supports and w L²/24 at mid-span. When the support sections yield they rotate at constant moment and the load is redistributed until a hinge also forms at mid-span. Equating the external work to the internal work of the three-hinge mechanism gives a capacity requirement of **M<sub>p</sub> = w L<sub>n</sub>² / 16** at every hinge. This moment has been used for the blast design of the perimeter walls, walls W6 and W7, the pressure slab, the headhouse roof and walls, with the sections kept under-reinforced so that the hinges can rotate.

P1 Flexural Capacity | The flexural capacity of every section has been computed from IS 456 Annex G-1.1(b), with the limiting moment from Annex G-1.1(c) and the neutral axis depth from Cl. 38.1.

#calc
M<sub>u</sub> = 0.87 f<sub>y</sub> A<sub>st</sub> d [1 - (A<sub>st</sub> f<sub>y</sub>) / (b d f<sub>ck</sub>)]
x<sub>u</sub> = 0.87 f<sub>y</sub> A<sub>st</sub> / (0.36 f<sub>ck</sub> b); x<sub>u,max</sub> / d = 0.46 for Fe500
M<sub>u,lim</sub> = 0.133 f<sub>ck</sub> b d²
#end

P1 Shear | Shear has been checked to IS 456 Cl. 40, with τ<sub>c</sub> from Table 19 at the static strength of concrete and τ<sub>c,max</sub> from Table 20. Where τ<sub>v</sub> exceeds τ<sub>c</sub>, links have been designed for V<sub>us</sub> = (τ<sub>v</sub> - τ<sub>c</sub>) b d with A<sub>sv</sub> / s<sub>v</sub> = V<sub>us</sub> / (0.87 f<sub>y</sub> d), subject to the maximum spacing of Cl. 26.5.1.5.

P1 Results Used in Design | The following results of the model have been used directly in design:-

P2 Base Shear | F<sub>x</sub> = 18.295 kN per column, total 73.18 kN, which is exactly the equal-share distribution assumed in the portal method.

P2 Seismic Axial Forces | F<sub>y</sub> = ±36.127 kN (EQ in X) and ±27.891 kN (EQ in Z) in the columns due to overturning. The larger value, 36.127 kN, has been used in the column and footing design.

P1 Portal Method | The seismic member forces have been worked out by the portal method at the model base shear of 73.18 kN, with the storey forces distributed in proportion to W h².

#calc
Q<sub>roof</sub> = 73.18 x (315.9 x 6.25²) / Σ W h² ;; 59.51 kN
Q<sub>floor</sub> = 73.18 x (276.8 x 3.20²) / Σ W h² ;; 13.67 kN
Column shear, ground storey = 73.18 / 4 ;; 18.30 kN
Column shear, first storey = 59.51 / 4 ;; 14.88 kN
Column moment, ground storey = 18.30 x 3.200 / 2 ;; 29.27 kNm
Column moment, first storey = 14.88 x 3.050 / 2 ;; 22.69 kNm
Beam moment at the first-floor joint = 29.27 + 22.69 ;; 51.96 kNm
Beam moment at the roof joint ;; 22.69 kNm
#end

T This distribution places slightly more shear in the ground storey than the model's own distribution (roof 55.57 kN, floor 17.61 kN), and gives the larger ground-storey column moment.
