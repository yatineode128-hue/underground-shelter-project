#chapter SITE SELECTION AND GEOTECHNICAL INVESTIGATION | (SITE, SOIL, ROCK, GROUNDWATER AND CLIMATE)

#head Site Selection

P1 Location | The site is located within the CTW area of the College of Military Engineering campus, Pune. It is an undeveloped rectangular plot east of the two CTW blocks, bounded on the east by the perimeter track and the nullah line, with the CBRN Live Training Area to the south. The location of the site is shown in Fig 2.1. The following inferences are drawn from it:-

P2 | The plot is open ground clear of the existing buildings and roads, so the excavation and the construction yard can be laid out without disturbing any existing facility.

P2 | The plot is separated from the CTW blocks by open ground, which reduces the effect of the noise and dust of rock breaking on the occupied buildings.

P2 | A track runs along the eastern side of the plot and gives access for plant and tippers.

P1 Significance of the Site Features | Four of the strengths are of direct value to the design. The existing services on the campus are shown in Fig 2.2.

P2 No Pipelines | The plot is free of buried services. Every service that crosses the protective envelope is a blast, gas and EMP discontinuity, and a clear plot allows the box to be excavated and tanked without diversions.

P2 Electric Supply | Mains supply is available on the campus from substation SS-15, and an existing electric line is available for the peacetime supply to the installation, with the generator and battery providing the protected supply.

P2 Water Supply | The campus water supply is fed from an RCC overhead reservoir of 15 000 gallons capacity on 15 m staging, and supplies the installation in peacetime (Chapter 12).

P2 Road Connectivity | About 1 000 m³ of rock is to be broken and removed by tipper, and the existing roads provide the haul route.

#head Site Reconnaissance

P1 Site as Found | The condition of the plot at the time of the site reconnaissance is shown in Fig 2.3. The following inferences are drawn:-

P2 | The plot is covered with scrub and grass, with loose basalt at the surface. There is no structure or large tree on it, and site clearance is limited to clearing and grubbing the scrub and stripping the topsoil (Appendix B).

P2 | The loose basalt at the surface agrees with the shallow rock recorded in the trial pits (Table 2.2).

P2 | Water stands on the track and in shallow depressions after rain, which shows that the surface drains slowly. The finished grade over the box is accordingly crowned and falls at 1 in 50, and a channel across the entrance of the covered stairwell intercepts surface water (Chapter 3).

P2 | An overhead electric line runs along the access track.

#head Sub-Soil Investigation

P1 Source of Data | The geotechnical data for the site is taken from the sub-soil investigation carried out by the Soil Engineering and Material Testing (SEMT) Wing, CME, Report No SEMT/67/15, *Report on Sub-Soil Investigation for CTW Ph-III ACCN Project at CME Pune*. Eleven trial pits were excavated by JCB at three locations adjoining the site (G Building, H Building and Mess Building), and soil samples and rock cores were tested in the laboratory. In addition, four confirmatory boreholes with rock coring at the location of the underground structure form the first item of the works (Appendix B) to confirm the founding strata before excavation.

P1 Geology | The report states that *"the area under investigation is underlain by the Deccan Trap formation. These are volcanic lava flows, which were poured out between the late cretaceous and early Eocene times. These basaltic flows are horizontally bedded and more or less uniform in character over a wide area."* The founding stratum of the underground structure is therefore sound basalt.

#head Surface Soil and Groundwater

P1 Black Cotton Soil | The surface soil at two of the three locations is a high plasticity clay (CH) with a free swell index of 60 to 65 per cent, which places it in the black cotton soil category. No structural element is founded in it. The underground structure bears on basalt at (-)6.800 and the sentry post footings on in-situ basalt at (-)2.000. Under the stepped raft of the covered entry stairwell, whose top is at about (-)0.300, the clay is stripped to 1.0 m below existing ground over the raft area plus 1.0 m beyond every edge and replaced with granular fill of free swell index not more than 20 per cent, compacted in 200 mm layers to at least 95 per cent of MDD.

P1 Groundwater | The investigation report records that *"water table was not encountered in any trial pit. However, water table may raise during/after rainy season."* The design groundwater table has therefore been taken at **(-)2.000**, i.e. 2.0 m below finished ground level, representing the monsoon condition. This is conservative for both the hydrostatic uplift on the mat and the lateral pressure on the walls. At this level the lateral load on the walls below the water table is about two-thirds water pressure and one-third effective earth pressure.

#head Excavation

P1 Excavation Profile | The excavation is 6.8 m deep to formation, with 1.0 m working space around the box at formation level. The soil and murrum cap above rockhead is battered at not steeper than 1 : 1 on all four sides, graded back to rockhead, and the face below rockhead is cut vertical in basalt.

P1 Method | Rock is broken by hydraulic breaker; blasting is not permitted, so that the founding rock and the adjoining campus facilities are not disturbed. The excavation is dewatered continuously from the start of excavation until the backfill and cover are complete.

P1 Treatment of Weak Seams | Basalt flows commonly contain red-bole or vesicular layers at flow contacts. Any such seam exposed at formation level is over-excavated and replaced with M15 lean concrete. The mat is designed for the loss of support over a 3.0 m wide band at the worst position (Chapter 7), so that a local seam does not govern the structure.
