D.15   FLOOR SCREED AGAINST THE MAT SIDL ALLOWANCE  -  the governing constraint
====================================================================================================

  The internal floor is the top of the 600 mat.  A drained floor needs a
  screed, and the screed is dead load on a mat that master A.7.2 allows only
  1.0 kPa of SIDL.  That allowance, not hydraulics, sizes the falls.

  Mat SIDL allowance                 1.0 kPa            [C] A.7.2
  Screed density                     24 kN/m3          [C] A.7.3 uses 24 for screed
  MAXIMUM AVERAGE SCREED             1.0 / 24 = 42 mm   [R]

  ADOPTED FLOOR GRADING - zone 1, bays 1 to 5, falling east to the sump:
      minimum screed at the sump edge                       25 mm   [A]
      longitudinal fall 1:400 over 11200            = 28 mm   [A]
      transverse fall 1:100 over 2500 each side       = 25 mm   [A]
      screed at the sump edge  25 mm  ->  at the far corner 78 mm   [R]
      AREA-AVERAGE SCREED                                   52 mm   [R]

  Screed load    52 mm x 24 kN/m3 = 1.24 kPa
  Allowance                                1.00 kPa
  EXCESS                                   +0.24 kPa = +24 %
  Over the 104.0 m2 internal floor          = +25 kN   [R]
  For scale: the mat itself weighs 2046 kN, and the hydrostatic
  uplift on it is 6289 kN (master B.3).  The excess is 1.2 % of the mat weight
  and 0.39 % of the uplift, and it acts in the FAVOURABLE
  direction for flotation.

  *** FINDING - DR-F4.  REFERRED, NOT ASSUMED. ***
      A drained floor cannot be built inside a 1.0 kPa SIDL allowance at
      24 kN/m3: 42 mm of screed is all the allowance buys, and 42 mm
      does not contain a fall over a 2.5 m run plus a minimum thickness at
      the outlet.  Three ways out, for the structural engineer to choose:
        (a) accept the +0.24 kPa (+25 kN) as a revision to the mat SIDL
        (b) use a lightweight or thin-set levelling system and re-check
        (c) reduce the falls, accepting standing water between washdowns
      THIS PACKAGE DRAWS (a) AND FLAGS IT.  It is a change to a stated
      design load in master A.7.2 and is not made silently.

  *** FINDING - DR-F5 ***
      Two of the three hydraulic zones have no closed drainage destination.
      This is not a layout error - it is a consequence of the protective
      boundary, and closing it is a PROTECTIVE-DESIGN decision (a blast and
      gas-tight boundary crossing, or manual transfer in sealed containers),
      not a drainage one.  This package:
        - drains zone 1 completely;
        - drains zone 2 to a collection point at the W6 side of bay 6 and
          marks the onward route to the bay 8 tank UNDEFINED on D-203;
        - provides threshold upstands at W5 and at blast door 1 so that no
          water can run from zone 2 or zone 3 into zone 1;   [A]
        - records zone 3 as having no destination.
      ENGINEER TO CONFIRM.  Nothing is assumed across the boundary.
