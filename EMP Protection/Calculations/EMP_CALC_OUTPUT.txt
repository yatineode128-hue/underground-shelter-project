E.6  EARTHING - AND WHY 5 OHM IS THE WRONG TARGET TO CHASE
==============================================================================

  Target      <= 5 ohm   [C] master G (IEEE 142 /
               IS 3043) and the WM1 confirmed electrical scope.
  Ground      Deccan basalt, 1000 - 10000 ohm.m  [C] master K.3,
               which already says "test earth resistance early".

  ONE ROD, 3 m x 16 mm   [A]
      R = rho/(2 pi L) [ ln(4L/a) - 1 ]

      rho =    1000 ohm.m   R =     334.9 ohm   rods for 5 ohm, NO interaction:     67
      rho =   10000 ohm.m   R =    3349.3 ohm   rods for 5 ohm, NO interaction:    670

      Rods interact.  A real group needs substantially more than the
      ideal count, and there is nowhere on this site to put them.
      ** 5 OHM IS NOT ACHIEVABLE WITH RODS IN BASALT. **  [D]

  THE STRUCTURE ITSELF, which is already built and already bonded
      Mat footprint            22.0 x 6.2 = 136.4 m2  [C] A.4.2
      Equivalent disc radius   sqrt(A/pi) = 6.589 m
      Surface-disc form, R = rho / 4r   (the conservative one; a fully
      buried disc is rho/8r, twice as good)

      rho =    1000 ohm.m   R =      37.9 ohm
      rho =   10000 ohm.m   R =     379.4 ohm

      Better than a single rod by a factor of 8.8, and it
      costs nothing because the cage is there.  A concrete-encased
      electrode is the right answer on rock.  [D]

  *** BUT THE IMPORTANT POINT IS THAT 5 OHM IS NOT AN EMP NUMBER. ***
      <= 5 ohm is a POWER-SAFETY and LIGHTNING requirement from IS 3043
      and IEEE 142.  It is real and it still applies.  It is not what
      makes an EMP shield work.  An EMP shield works by being
      EQUIPOTENTIAL, and equipotential is decided by BONDING INDUCTANCE,
      not by earth resistance.  E.7 shows why.  [D]

E.7  BONDING - WHERE EMP DESIGN ACTUALLY LIVES
==============================================================================

  L = 2e-7 . l . [ ln(2l/(w+t)) + 0.5 + 0.2235 (w+t)/l ]   henries

                             STRAP   l mm  w mm     L nH   X @1MHz  X @10MHz  X @100MHz
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
       LONG ROUND-THE-CORNER STRAP    600    25    512.2     3.22o    32.18o    321.82o
     HOUSE RULE - SHORT WIDE STRAP    100    50     38.9     0.24o     2.45o     24.46o
           HOUSE RULE - LIMIT CASE    150    50     69.4     0.44o     4.36o     43.59o
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  A 600 mm strap is 322 ohm at 100 MHz.  That is
  not a bond.  It is a resistor with a nice green sleeve on it.
  Shortening it to 100 mm and widening it to 50 takes the inductance
  from 512 nH to 39 nH, a factor of 13.2.

  THE RULES THAT FOLLOW, and they are the ones that get built wrong: [D]
    - every bond <= 100 mm long
    - width : length at least 5 : 1
    - FLAT STRAP ONLY.  Never a round wire, never a pigtail, never a
      'we'll loop it round to the nearest stud'
    - clean bare metal both ends, protected after making
    - the shield bonds to the structure at ONE place.  A second bond is
      a loop, and a loop is an antenna.

  This is the same physics the project already relies on without saying
  so: master A.5 requires the blast door frames to be CAST IN AND WELDED
  TO THE CAGE  [C], and B.7.2 requires it even for the non-blast-rated
  headhouse door.  Those are EMP bonds.  They were specified correctly.

E.8  VERIFICATION - THE ONLY THING THAT SETTLES ANY OF THIS
==============================================================================

  IEEE Std 299 is in the register  [C] for a shielding effectiveness
  survey.  Applied here:

    EMP ZONE 2   SURVEY IT.  Full IEEE 299 survey, 10 kHz - 1 GHz, on
                 the completed enclosure with every penetration made
                 off and every panel closed.  Acceptance 80 dB.  This
                 is a HOLD POINT: no equipment installed before it
                 passes, because a failed survey means opening seams.

    EMP ZONE 1   DO NOT SURVEY IT, AND DO NOT CLAIM IT.  A buried box
                 under 2 m of engineered cover cannot be surveyed to
                 IEEE 299 - there is no accessible exterior to put a
                 transmitter on.  E.2 is a calculation, and it will
                 stay a calculation.  Any statement that the box gives
                 80 dB is unsupportable and should not be made.  [D]

    WHAT CAN BE CHECKED ON SITE, cheaply, during construction:
      - CONTINUITY of the cage across every construction joint, before
        the pour.  Master F.1 already requires two waterstops and a
        welded Cu/galvanised EMP strap at every joint  [C].  MEASURE IT.
      - CONTINUITY of every cast-in frame to the cage, before the pour.
      - EARTH RESISTANCE, early, as K.3 already demands  [C].
      - BOND RESISTANCE at every strap after making off.
    None of these prove shielding effectiveness.  All of them catch the
    mistakes that destroy it, at the only moment they can be fixed.

  4  [N] VENDOR DATA
        No shielded door, PCI, honeycomb panel, blast valve or blast door
        vendor data. Blast door RF performance is unknown, and it sits on
        the protective boundary.

  5  [N] MATERIALS
        No pipe material anywhere in the drainage package; no concrete
        permittivity or conductivity; no rebar crossing treatment (tied or
        welded) stated anywhere.

  6  [U] BOUNDARY
        Whether bay 8 is inside the EMP boundary has never been decided,
        because no EMP boundary has ever been drawn. E.4 proposes one; only
        the client can adopt it.

  7  [U] SENTRY POST
        Excluded, consistent with every other services package. It is above
        ground and not blast designed; its EMP exposure is total and nothing
        here changes that.

  NO VALUE IN THIS PACKAGE RESOLVES AN EXISTING [ASSUMED], [UNRESOLVED]
  OR [NOT AVAILABLE] ITEM.  Six new open items EM-V1 to EM-V6 are
  raised in the design basis.  That is deliberate: a package that
  quietly filled these in would be inventing a specification.
